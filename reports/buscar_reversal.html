What buscar measures

buscar (Bioactive Unbiased Single-cell Compound Assessment and Ranking) scores each perturbation on two numbers, both measured against one anchor: the healthy (mock) population, buscar's target. The on-score is the distance from mock in the features that actually change between healthy and diseased cells, scaled so the diseased control (active_untreated, buscar's reference) sits at exactly 1.0 — a low on-score means strong reversal toward healthy. The off-score asks: does the treatment also change features that should not move? A low off-score means the treatment is specific, not just generally disruptive to the cell.

Why this report scores wells, not single cells

buscar needs the mock and disease control populations to actually look different before it can score anything. The pipeline checks this automatically before running buscar (a biological QC gate, using each feature's Cohen's d effect size). At the single-cell level, cell-to-cell variation drowns out the treatment signal in this pilot. Aggregating to one row per well first, the same two populations separate clearly.

Efficacy vs. specificity

Each point is one treatment group. The x-axis is the off-score (lower is more specific); the y-axis is the on-score (lower is more reversed toward healthy). The bottom-left corner is where a good treatment should land: strong reversal, few side effects.

Dose response

Remdesivir is the pilot's known-active drug (expected to reverse the infection phenotype at higher doses); Oseltamivir carboxylate is a known-inactive drug (it targets a different virus family, not coronaviruses). A working buscar score should show Remdesivir's reversal getting stronger (on-score dropping) as the dose increases, and Oseltamivir staying flat and weak across doses. (Note these are assay-validation drug controls, distinct from buscar's own two control populations — mock is the healthy target, active_untreated the diseased reference.)
