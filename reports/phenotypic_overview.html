HRCE-1, Plate 25 · 42 wells · 168 sites · 33,330 single cells · RxRx19a pilot run

What this pilot set out to check

RxRx19a asks whether a treatment can move a SARS-CoV-2-infected cell's shape and texture back toward how a healthy cell looks. This page compares two ways of measuring "how a cell looks": CellProfiler's hand-designed features, and Recursion's deep-learning site embeddings, published with the dataset. If both methods agree on which sites look alike, that is evidence the pilot's morphology signal is real and not a processing artifact.

Do the two representations agree?

Two plain, boring metrics compare site-to-site geometry directly (plan section 17), without relying on how the UMAP plots above happen to look.

Spearman correlation of site-distance matrices
—
1.0 = identical site ordering, 0 = no agreement

This compares every pair of sites: does CellProfiler say two sites are close when Recursion's embedding also says they are close? A value above 0.5 means the two methods broadly agree on site similarity, even though they use completely different features.

Mean k-nearest-neighbor overlap (k = 10)
—
fraction of each site's 10 closest neighbors shared

For each site, this checks its 10 closest sites in each feature space, then counts how many neighbors the two methods have in common. Random chance with 168 sites would give about 0.06. A higher number is stronger local agreement.

MorphEm: single-cell deep-learning representation

MorphEm (Caicedo Lab) is a deep-learning model that embeds each cell directly from its five-channel crop image, no hand-designed features at all. It ran on the same 33,330 segmented cells in this pilot, on Alpine CPU nodes, inside its own Apptainer container. Each cell gets a 5 × 384 = 1,920-dim embedding (one 384-dim vector per channel).
